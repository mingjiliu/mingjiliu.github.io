One poem written in 2023 (also I wrote none in 2024). All poems by Mingji Liu.

## Himalayan poems

*2023 · Mingji Liu*

9-4-23

Apprehension  
alights as my flight dips into Lord Buddha's birthplace  
what have I done?  
Touching down into this undated, hilly night/

10-4-23

A buffalo died for my momos today. This much is true.  
Then I found a little durbar whiskey, a perfect amber sin,  
and felt much better about it.

11-4-23

I clockwise the stupa, white as the soul of a mountain.  
The earth turns about me and I must bow in turn.  
(somebody must sell cheap crampons in thamel)

12-4-23

Dear diary, today on the bus a Nepali  
woman told me I was handsome.  
That almost makes up for eight ashen  
honking hours, rumbling down a  
serpent of madness complete.

13-4-23

Waterfalls. Wild marijuana.  
Ngadi and Ghermu and Jagat.  
Village names that roll like fruits in one's  
mouth.  
Like these pomegranate seeds, redder than  
Lali Gurans.

14-4-23

Wild beehives on the trail to Tal, stalactited to the  
cliff walls. Clinging to the idea of  
a valley. its winds, rock slides,  
tree groans.

15-4-23

Mutt's acapella upon blackest  
Chame  
A cold, fitful sleep.

16-4-23

Apple juice flavoured with massif views  
Ice-wept waters feeding five-coloured flowers  
Prayer wheels a'turning while my dal bhat's churning.

17-4-23

my hair is seabuckthron, my lungs' jeep dust,  
my skin gompa plaster, my bones kindling stacks.  
my thoughts are street dogs -- loitering, roofless  
but my eyes, these are the frozen peaks themselves,

growing bigger, ever bigger

18-4-23

> Acclimatising to high breeze, tight blood,  
> bad breakfasts.  
> A holding pattern of cinnamon rolls, lama blessings and handwrung  
> socks.  
> The vulture crowns this glacial sky, silent,  
> gazing,  
> patron saint of traveller's patience.

19-4-23

Three pale trekkers smaller  
than the furthest cloud, suspended on a frost-kissed bridge.

Juniper leaves, alpine brush  
bosomed in this glacial rest.

Horse hooves... mala chants...  
fading with the herder's return.

20-4-23

Ode to a yak! Rocking to unseen currents.  
Hairy old soul of the hilltops, grassward stooping,  
his face is rugged, wetly lyrical.  
He's the blues musician of Thorung La, soloist of snowfields,  
watch him compose the slushfall with his  
musk, his grunts, his gravity.

21-4-23

The big day. A cold champa.  
Trudged and trudged and  
into the snowstorm and into  
the dawn and into the  
mountain's claws and into  
the fluids of my living heart,  
of my brain's dazed pulse,  
and we kept on going and kept  
on going. Eventually settling -- into  
that holy temple where we  
crawled on our knees to see  
its eternal flame.

22-4-23

Searing skin hot spring near-plunge driving  
walking half-frosted shit sludge slipping  
jagged steps a pint of beer stoner chatter  
with fresh landslides  
  
Is it no wonder I meditated  
so poorly this evening?

23-4-23

Ghorepani means  
"horse water", it means  
the micro-tears in my quads  
as I sweated up ancient steps that  
a baby goat could dance. It means  
the sullen rhododendron forest, waiting for  
next year's spring, it means thunder flashings  
the false promise of rains. It means an apple pie,  
as American as the grinning Americans around the  
fireplace where the porters sat also, wondering how  
many strides further, away from their village away from  
their back and neck muscles. It means the water buffalo  
that grazed an improbable angle above us, ruminating with  
its cud and its beautiful polished eyes.

24-4-24

Three-thousand steps to Hile  
in the drizzle. Three-thousand knee bends.  
Three-thousand ankle creaks -- potential slips --  
thoughts -- daydreams -- three-thousand  
lusts, frights, tightenings, rages, loves rushing  
dead-on to chin slam nose crack spine tumble down  
three-thousand more mindless roads not and never taken.

25-4-23

Nayapul, the end began to unveil itself,  
ribboning out in a jeep to Pokhara, from good road then bad;  
DIGESTIVES (the healthy choice), JASMINE PAINTS;  
from farm to river to town and more town  
till the world became an unbearable village once more.

A fire atop the mountain briefly  
illuminates.  
Embers, orange fadings, are but echoes  
of people come and gone.

26-4-23

Our walk, finale, guideless,  
along Pokhara's jungle spine to Shanti Stupa.  
Peaceable glow in a peaceless process.  
  
I napped all afternoon in a dingy bed /  
From what exhaustion? From where? The heat?  
The food? / Or from grief and joy of letting them  
go from my palms?
