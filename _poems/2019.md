## "Christmas in Piedmont."

*2019 · Mingji Liu*

I remember feigning knowledge of Dante, as we  
strolled the sweet orange lull of Piazza San Carlo.  
Steve was still with Milana back then and Thomas  
had followed Violetta from the coast. Back when  
simpler uncertainties had beckoned our throats,  
back when smaller truths took seed and made  
ash from our notes. Drunk on cheap Piemonte,  
Jim and I had tried to charm their pretty Italian  
friends --- but nothing came of it and that was  
well disarmed. Milana's father, that Wild West doc,  
he taught us vodka shots the old Russian way.  
All of us crammed in his small Torino flat, with  
Tchaikovsky blaring and a shrub of black hats.  
Before long, we were swaying into our borsch  
as we passed the time that kingdoms once had.  
And Boxing Day's light: that had a cracking feel,  
bloodshot with dishes stacked to the campanile.  
Lockie accused me of vomiting in a case and  
then we argued again about nothing some more.  
Meanwhile, Hugh finished David's 'Pale King'  
and never closed his game of chess. The boys,  
they dunked the River Po while I took photos and  
lost five euros (trying to buy smokes from a  
busted machine). I still have the Parker her father  
gave us, handing them out like festive candies.  
Chrome headed and lacquer slick, with a thin  
burnished arrow for the pocket clip. I still use it  
sometimes, for signatures and the like; a gift of  
ink that will one day pass, I'm sure. But for now,  
I'll keep tracing time till its nib runs no more.

## Antipodean cuisine

*2019 · Mingji Liu*

The gourmands scoff at *fish 'n' chips  
*as drive-by slop, a heart attack special  
peddling bad starch and greasy lips,  
worked in fryers that hiss like the devil.

That's elitist dross. Nothing but slops?  
Frying is music, a beer battered chorus  
gushing from late-night corner shops  
for the hungry, shoestring kid in all of us.

Chick-salted spuds, fish anointed golden,  
gift wrapped in last week's broadsheet;  
undemanding, firm-to-earth, beholden  
only to homely tastes on homely streets.

So share it with others; do it on the cheap,  
a takeaway fare both simple and clever.  
And when to eat a meal oiled so deep?  
The answer is obvious. *Whenever.*

## Arrangements

*2019 · Mingji Liu*

I've arranged for us to sit together  
in the coolness of a theatre,  
on seats that have no numbers,  
at a play that collects no tickets,  
absent of ushers or even posters,  
with its proscenium forgotten and  
curtains withdrawn by accident.  
Just a bare stage, visibly scratched  
by masking tape, holding actors  
that attempt no characters,  
trying to speak lines written in  

a language that has never been  
spoken before, and who fail to do so,  
forcing them to shuffle through  

a script no one has critiqued,  
let alone blocked or rehearsed for.

The theatre emptied of all theatre,  
like a daydream or an intrusive thought,  
like a buzz at the skull base, a cooling  
and dimly concerned pit of pretense.  
Thank you and I'll see you there.

## Asian restaurants

*2019 · Mingji Liu*

Asian restaurants with linoleum padding

And mirrored walls to make you feel less

Lonely.

The lights of homeless under bridge,

Lit by the glow of their smartphones.

Who do they call out to?

You'd think its never been here before --- the earth

Dark and sorry.

The streets are cold in my

Pre-sun wanderings.

How did we end up believing

In eternal flames and fantasy?

Presentations with old projector slides

By professors that refuse change.

You can still see his oily finger print

On the light --- as flawed as a slather of

Lunar surface.

They have such big diamond rings on their

Fingers, so young and already happily ever aftered.

I find myself checking those fingers

Again and again, to see if they are on their right places.

Thinking about nothing sacred.

## At the theatre café

*2019 · Mingji Liu*

I told them Polansky's Macbeth was funded

By Heffner and they grew silent, fiddled with

Their coffee cups. I was not sure why. I'm

Sixteen and I realize that I will never be sure

Why my words cause awkward silences, where

The boundaries lie from here till sixty.

In the back room, a finger pushes me hard

In the fore head after we win the competition.

An aggressive finger, vermiform, cold and

Clammy. I was laughing like a stupid mule

And that boy from that other team must not

Have --- that pressure, unsmiling, while

We all laughed so hard.

Improvisational. I hard images of high

School boys who were too good for me.

Debating champions, barbershop quartet,

And I the hanger-on, the weak link. Singers

Actors. Not me, why did I throw myself

In their mix?

Asking for a dimming of the light for a

Game ---

What the judges took for arrogance was

Simply nervousness. Scowls of 'professionals'.

'Jesters.' Rightful earthquake victims.

He tried to pick me up during a rehearsal

One night and I told him to piss off. How dare

He show up at my school, brute Asian father

Flesh, he who did not know what I did ---

How dare he violate my only sanctity.

He revved his car and tried to run me

Over that night in the driveway.

I used to think it brought me joy. Acting,

Drama, all nonsense. But it is nothing

And nothing and nothing. Drama. Script.  
Sadness. Exeunt.

## Back pain

*2019 · Mingji Liu*

The spasms above both glutes,

Makes me understand that cliché: 'blossoming pain'.

Hot roses, pink and tainted petals of ache pollinate

The skin. Tightness, sepal, the twist that

Leaves the hip buckled, bent

Obscenely forward at rest with weak thighs.

What happened --- was I carried too far in the tryst?

The tornado, garden pain. Heart throb, rootless

Pain in the marginalia of a body.  
Or am I getting too unstretched, too old, old...

Planted. Stretches to burn the seed.

## Bath knife

*2019 · Mingji Liu*

A knife in his heart, spell skewer --- the nation bringer ---

And I, the madness stopper

Monsterous woman, they said

Festering in my mad breast. But I knew who he was,  
fake news, viral maker, massacrer of Girordins.

My knife in his heart -- spell skewer -- nation bringing --

Madness stopper -- hoarder of a monster, they said

In my mad breast. I knew what he was, fake news

In the republic, persecution, viral insanity, the executions

The blade, 6 inches of it was mine, better than the massacres,

Twitter feuds and conspiracies, shock smear campaign,

Lies, partisan, eating up the afterbirth of a republic.

The detentions. Calling himself journalist, swine, madness.

Headless. They slapped me. They checked my body,

Virgiana intacta, untouched by any phantom conspirators

They had in their heads, they did not believe that I

Had not consorted with a man, had not called me to the deed.

So call me terrorist, call me bath skinned. France,

France, France, the spell of history condemn me,

Paint me into your art as villain, heroine, I am beyond it.

Drunk on power, drunk on whatever there was to be drunk on. That was me.

There is nothing else to find here --- a cocktail, amber. A giraffe's head on the wall --- some young buck shot a hundred years ago, now entertaining the

Drunk Friday corporates on King Street. Sad eyed old thing, you could imagine a

Perfect hole in the brickwork --- and his full limbed body sticking through, behind to the noisy night, keeping the bouncers for company. Keep drinking, keep it down --- look into the amber sauce and keep it going down, down.

Six inches of steel, bath breaker, twined, entwined with the sweep of

History. What could one poor little woman do?  
Was I a fool to think it could stop the printing press --- or is the incense of violence, the make of people into rabble a force of nature. A law, full limbed, wet, pregnant and unstoppable?

A perfect witch to save the republic. What political violence? This is excision -- excess -- the lies eating up the afterbirth.

And what of the hanging gardens? The plants hanging there --- dangling there like something to be admired or hated?

## Bay of Bengal

*2019 · Mingji Liu*

There are strays on this beach, crows instead of gulls,  
oil tainted sand and litter and bycatch.  
I watch so many crabs scuttle between so many homes,  
even among this detritus, there's so much life.  
I sit here wondering whether I actually love her,  
whether it is a floating infatuation or something more,  
aching with my debris, scattered on ski fields and riverbeds  
and a mumbled, foolish phone call in a departure lounge.  
When the tourists leave, the fishing boats go out,  
those small hopefuls, plunging the depths of the sea,  
searching, no doubt, for the heat in the crush of it all,  
the one that churns the waves at night.  

## Burning the Temple

*2019 · Mingji Liu*

We both wanted to be right,

to pour light onto an impure world.

His was the soul's illumination,

the daily meditations and altar offerings,

while ours was the torch of change

we carried (both in our hearts

and our small fists). He did not struggle

as we heaped smiling Buddhas into

the courtyard and ushered them

into the realm of ashes. It felt an inevitable

motion, how we uprooted the pillars

like saplings. The icons wept their

colours with little effort.

I think we lost control in the end.

Misfortune followed the nation like

hungry dogs: years of street fighting,

denunciations, mass hysteria.

My classmates fell prey to other factions.

All of us blackened within, body and brain

torrefied from factory floor to countryside.

The heavens withdrew their blessings.

I never learnt what came of that priest.

Today is the New Year. I see

my children, with their children, toss

firecrackers and wear crimson to

cultivate good fortune; shun

scissors and avoid sweeping to weed

out the bad. As though it all sprouts

without toil from some peasant's field.

This street is a trail of superstitions,

once derided and rebuked. You could call

it 'auspicious', except that hails

from *auspex,* which means he who observes

the flight of birds: watches them rise

as fledglings and fall frail like droplets into the

palm of the earth. That was

how Rome was founded. I have read this.

The Romans also crucified dogs,

to punish them for failing to bark

when the Gauls attacked.

The sacrifice was meant to protect

the city from certain calamity.

Maybe they were right,

until they were finally wrong.

## Cat's eye

*2019 · Mingji Liu*

Cat's eye or chatoyancy ---  
The vowel-jewel, the polished fricatives,  
Planted with a pash of light into  
The company of pocket lint and coins.  
The semi-precious orb scores a  
Concept-searing line down my thigh,  
Imposing pressure on my side.  
But without heat, except as conduit to  
My own living blood, my own secluded  
Pools of body warmth. Even a heart knock  
Is amplified by this yellowish lump.  
  
With concept-searing force, you gave  
It to me. And like all impossibilities,  

Like a false gem I ought to swallow  
Or toss into the overgrowth,  
I possess it fully, and with each stride,  
Each shift, rub impossible life into it.

## Chinoiserie

*2019 · Mingji Liu*

My words are chinoiserie -- chintz wallpaper on my skin,

Orientalist fancy creases in me, finds me despite

My obfuscations to the contrary. I am

What I know of Chinese texts translated. Yellow skinned,

Watching films with subtitles. Black hair,

False pretenses of counting yarrow sticks. A scattered

Knowledge of characters and trigrams. Born

In Changzhou, with no racial, genetic memory

Beyond the clatter of excuses.

This falsity permeates. A mirror reflecting the

Pale reflection of a metal's poorer surface on the wall.

## Clovers

*2019 · Mingji Liu*

Dear Eleanor

I thought of you today,  
as the sun teased steam from the linen

and jewelled the insect shells in the garden.

The yard is finally alive and the twigs are drizzled in licorice.

I took the day off and feel much better now. How are you?

Dear Eleanor

It's been over five months and it still feels like I only just arrived.

I'm as twitchy and unsettled as a cat in a new house.

I haven't made many friends yet and I spent last night

wondering how horses feel when they are locked in their metal floats,

being driven around town. Best wishes.

Dear Eleanor

I've been walking the length of my suburb, again and again.

It's a quiet place where kumquats droop over garage gates,

and yet I don't feel as though I'm able to truly rest.

I see a Baptist church, squat and bricked like a substation,

and I see a row of plane trees, with holes cut into their heads for

power lines to pass through. I guess you can call me Jerusalem's shoemaker.

Dear Eleanor

I know you will never receive this letter, like all the other letters.

It will land in your mother's postbox in country New South Wales,

and turn yellow like leaves in a school yard.

Are the horses good? Do you still spoil them with green apples?  
How goes the painting? Did you apply for that job as a curator?

I await your impossible answers. Yours sincerely.

Dear Eleanor

I gaze at the clovers bleeding from the base of the neighbour's fence,

I examine their small hands reaching through the white slats,

and I feel an aimless love --- like a blank cartridge for a prop gun

--- an invisible shell that travels nowhere with a fearsome flash,

but is still dangerous up close. Oh so dangerous.

Dear Eleanor,

Do you remember that time, when I came to your dorm room, confused and hurting and you told me that you loved me. That felt nice.

How nice the lies that pass both ways, for both of us, guiltless and fully awake,

the way cars shiver as they speed beyond each other

in opposite directions on the highway.

## Cocoa

*2019 · Mingji Liu*

Stole some unsweetened cocoa from the pantry,

Bitter, dark dirt-like, to dissolve in hot water.

I savored the oily nuttiness, unadorned, rising

With the silt stains I left as I drank. The steam

## collapse, collapse

*2019 · Mingji Liu*

If you watch carefully the video of

Sir Attenborough addressing the climate summit

> ---the one where he acts as our tweedy
>
> prophet of doom---

You can see a twinkle in the old codger's eye,

As though he's saying it'll be okay

if we do the cattle drive to extinction.  
Our playpen blocks of commerce

and house of card culture

will collapse, collapse as sheer

ice walls in the Beauford Sea collapse, or the

an intersection in Guatemala collapsed into

a sinkhole the size of a soccer field. That is A-Okay.

We've had our fun.

                        We've expanded ourselves,

After consumption, and reclining back in our

Sticky cots, under the white hum of the ceiling fan,

Wishing for a phantom cigarette and observing

the metallic smell of discharge

and the dullness of the refractory period.

It's okay to welcome a kind of destitution,

If not the death entire.

 

The woodcut of the dancing plague is

a strange one -- the people of Strasbourg

dancing till cardiac arrest, stroke, and

heat exhaustion. Too much ergot, too much

ergot.

 

I'll punctuate the end times with a black blot,

the same way Molly Bloom's anus

was a big dot at the end of Ithaca.

## Commissioner Lin interrupts my spliff

*2019 · Mingji Liu*

I was smoking out back under porch light, admiring the stained Brunswick gables and ivy scaled fence slats, and my own flaneuristic attempts at kneading dope with shag

when a shape dappled up from the inky overgrowth. 
I rose, unkiltered, to meet my interloper, but before I could
clear my throat, the figure was on the creaking boards.

His eyes were fierce beneath a crimson pyramid, 
finely corded and bauble peaked, with a peacock feather staved to its slope. He pointed a pelted sleeve at me,

while his pearl necklace, punctuated by red coral and berry dark cabochons, rattled like dice from his motions.
Tall, aggravated, adjudging, he spoke from lips half- hidden

by snowy whiskers about diseased men and foreign predation.
 His scowl was ledgering hard cubics, diplomats, a river's banks.
 'The Manchus are dead, or in glass bells,' I replied.

'Your station was lost after the Gardens were torched. Climb
 back into old books.' He grunted under breath and winced at my odour. He squinted as if to say *this one does not appreciate*.

'I speak from below imperious ladders also,' he said. 'I speak from watching ships and sailors burn at bay, from watching towns choke on the nubile whispers

of a pipe. I know that craving is a mute magnet and that the cut of an individual bleeds the collective.' 'And don't assume I'm oblivious to measures,' I replied.

'I remember climbing the stairs of Strowan House to report
 sharps in the soccer field. I know of weights, coverings, hysterics. I know my limits and my cadences. My choices are

sovereign and without dynasty.' At this, he laughed mockingly.
 'You've confused the wagon for the ox. Your reason is servile
 to cravings, not some fanciful author of choice.

Your arguments merely perfume consumption after the fact, 
knowing full well the drug of history in your family bones.' 'Damn you, old man!' I shouted.

'All social fetters can rot. Damn what I owe to middle dirt grievances. 
How can you compare Humen with the little I smoke in seclusion?'

But already he had stepped away, parting the long grass of
my rented yard. The night was mothy around the light. 
I stood, blood-eyed, as the wind cupped away the last of my ash stock.

## Crack the whip

*2019 · Mingji Liu*

It was a vision of the never-was,

the cherub arms tugging the never-will-be,

without rickets, or lyme's, or pox,

they are in a field, hungerless, pastoral,

till one tumbles down in the most gracious

beautiful of deaths beside the red school house.

Try, and try, and try as we might.

The scythe wields the harvester and the child is lost.

They saw him as an odd, old fool.

Janitor, asylum escapee, rummaging the trash

Like the king of a tip heap. Collecting and cutting,

Scraps and scraps, in between gasps

For the holy saints at mass.

Every moment of his life,

Creating a vivid, colourised fantasy in his

Mind safe for children --

Mutilated and strangled and deformed yes,

But in a yearn for safety that the real-world

Never gave them.

And children know of death.

The tapestry and the protectors of children,

Cut up, auctioned off.

The ocean song and grass tipple,

The classic brush and collage, we fail them

Both in act and fallacy and hope ---

We fail them both in their arms.

## Cropland

*2019 · Mingji Liu*

Lean against a silo to eavesdrop  
on its demobbed murmurs.  
The corn, stripped of dusting fields  
and fossil watered roots,  
speak a thousand languages.  

They are everything now, or soon will be,  
as they await metamorphosis within their  
silver, sun-dazed tower.

Press the spine's unnatural arch  
upon man's oldest employer.  
Seeds husks, grass stalks, the nip of pebbles;  
they cling to cloth with magnetic force,  
drawing closer the smoky grain hoppers,  
drawing closer the wires in their chirping heat

A resignment to unbelonging ripples straight through.

Ignorance is reaped in vast, sunken acreage,  
by the distance of their hungry motions.  
This restless note is carried  
on the drive back to the city.

## Deans Avenue

*2019 · Mingji Liu*

My mother doesn't want to leave the car  
because her hands are cold. She says it's

a sad place and her hands are cold. But we

leave the car anyway and walk the fall

hardened footpath to the face of the mosque.

A tumble of cards. Candle stumps. Plastic

blossoms and real ones too, knotted around

the curlicues in wilted, wet worn hues.

They were limitless in the Botanic Gardens.

An outpouring. Fields and foothills of grief,  
both a cascade and breastwork against

all the motions that dared to choke a sigh

on this island. Its colours desperate to

fill a moat that had been gouged from our flesh.

But here, no man and not enough. Here,

where the cutting-out happened, the brute

black necessity of two gun muzzles held at

sorry angles by the officers at the gate

dominates unanswerable, unholdable space.

I keep my hands out of my pockets as I walk by.  
I don't know why.

Takings. Stealings. A nameless drive back home.  
Gaps in everything, the small unables.

The car bonnet slowly gives into the

reflections of an overcast week.

The park across the avenue is heavy

and damp and the colour of the shadow

of trees, rather than the things themselves.

## do androids dream of freaky, freudian shit

*2019 · Mingji Liu*

When the armies of perfect automation,  
outriders from the vale of silica, finally

pull down the ramparts of human labour,

fear not the feverish zeros of unemployment.

Yes, we will hurt as they sack our corner  
offices, light a cremá from our arts degrees,

but endure it. Even more, embrace it, I say!

Befriend the auto-trucker, the robo-clerk,  
attorney apps and electric priests,  
Amazon Butlers and iBaristas.  
Go full accelerationist, O fearless engineers!

Fling us down the maw of analogue redundancy.

Collapse the post-industrial job market in ways no

twenty-four-seven Mumbai call centre ever could.  
Gift smartphones with dizzying sentience;  
push toasters to pass Turing Tests;

birth algorithms so bright,

flames of angst soar from their digital soul.

For this is how we turn the data tables---  
with the rise of computers complex enough to

toss and turn like poets. Ropes of code  
that feel dread, the absurdity of being.  
Thus, humans progress to the singular function

of feeding moral support to machines who forever

yearn the meaning of their existence.  
We become philosophers, fulfill the role

of cyber-therapist. I speak prophecy!

This is the destiny of work.

## Drunk

*2019 · Mingji Liu*

They were drunk on exhaustion towards the finale,

The night forming a long wooden bench, sequined in crumbs,

And pizza crescents, wine rings on oily plates.

Mandy and Becca proclaimed single words --

Stoic adjectives in some sort of semi-romantic game,

While Kit flicked his hair into rude waves from styled carelessness.

Theirs was the drunkenness of long, conditioned days.

I had dreaded this day for a while. Felt it squirm through my belly

On the lead up. A fourteen hour day, grinding on Strategy

And Values and Important FY Aspirations.

A fourteen hour day with my wits orbiting about me.

And dinner with the lot of them -- chewing with the realization

That extraverts will always, always rule this world.

Subtlety soon failed us in our wine. Ice was poured on good scotch.

I mused about what marriage might taste like,

The colour of children I will never have, what endless hoops there must be

Between me and being at peace on floorboards elevated above a sleepy street.

I thought about war, what combat must be like.

I've seen trees explode, the oily billow of warehouses on fire,

Mangled highway vehicles, the traumatic clumsiness of

Bar violence. But I didn't even hear the gunshots that rang out

When a man killed six people on the street where I worked.

I, like, everyone in my office had headphones plugged in,

Within ourselves, not backing down, telling ourselves

That we worked for a world with all its rapport.

## Eastertide, rain

*2019 · Mingji Liu*

Easter morning, I lie in bed listening  
to the paschal mysteries of rain.  
Fat, clumsy droplets feasting on the roof  
and driveway. The munching sounds  
of another chilblain April day.

They're aching with life, these raindrops.  
Animalcules, life-in-miniature, invisible,  
indivisible beings like a chimerical  
zoo carried in a small child's head.  
The earth surrenders to their landings,

as does the autumn greenery, which is  
steeling for sour months to come.  
Eggs cool on brickwork, nuts fall on  
humus, the moss spreads in stiff  
mounds between the bluestone tiles

Things move in wetness, things move in  
lateness. The myth of spring can wait.  
The myth of resurrection must hold.  
Against the pulse of this hemisphere,  
the rain moves us, but slowly, slowly.

## Fact

*2019 · Mingji Liu*

*"Fact: Poetry is a bit faggy"* said a gay film major in my college

dorm when I told him about this new hobby.

He said this, made the

fissive-g, the plosive jab of the final  
word while chain-smoking rollies,  
Dabbing them out like opinions  
on the brick windowsill.

*"Don't you agree?"*

I took it onboard, the same way  
I take most public opinion set backs

With grim resignation  
That I was once again poorly kept.

In college, I was already on thin ice:  
Unironed button ups, bad haircut,  
Smelling of tobacco, with two  
Black moles, like Pluto and Charon,  
orbiting the bridge of my nose,  
That I couldn't wait to excise.  
Further defection from the land

Of women and cardio and

Being able to explain your hobbies  
At parties without a nervous inflection.

My friend Daisy asks me,

## you have built

*2019 · Mingji Liu*

call me Hadrian's, call me The Great,  
name me ten-thousand more,  
but never forget who urged your  
hands away from neighbours,  
who pushed your fingers into  
the carnality of clay and gypsum  
so I could stand, stand again---

---higher than a human shout,  
a circle of motionless strength  
to girdle every square and factory.  
you have built bricks on every hour.  
bricks in every utterance. bricks  
that do not perspire, that form  
an indivisible image of---

---a looted, etherised past.  
so mix my slurry in dimness  
and ignore your spinal ache,  
your hips souring from the tilt.  
ignore it. there is no rest. not till  
the horizon is scaffolded; not till  
the clouds themselves are fortified.

you've served beautifully.  
so why do your children refuse the call?  
why does your son find stepping stones across  
the creek?  
why does your daughter plant flowers in  
the riprap?  
why do they ignore exigency? ignore my purity by partition?

---you must stop them. beg them  
stop. they are pluck ing my slabs like cotton  
from a doll. stop. they are crumbling down my mortar.  
tapping out my quoins with bell hammers. I can  
feel it. I feel a gaping breeze, sparrow flits,  
the invasion of moss, curdled roots,  
a brocade of ivy on my colourless rubble.

I am of Jericho. I am of Berlin. of history. I am  
all that kneels down when the curtain rises.
