## What is a pier but a disappointed bridge?

*2015 · Mingji Liu*

she stares out at

the Slovakian mountains

in beauty

her eyes kissed

with the lightest crow's feet

her hair, oily

chocolate ropes

she holds her mother's whip

she swings her father's hips.

This woman in

A half-torn shirt

Gazes out into the pinescape.

She runs from home.

What can I do?

With neither the

Long, white arms to hold

Or tall, dark beard to stroke.

She is wandering down

The graffiti alleys of

Lebanon.

The sandy shores of

Egypt.

In Jordan

She climbs a rock.

Buys her first shisha pipe.

In Jordan,

She meets a French man,

Coming down from Darwin through to Adelaide.

They agree to meet in six months time

At St Cross Station.

She drives her to Northern Victoria

Where they make love in

A motel room

In some dusty

Town in Northern Victoria.

What is a pier but

A disappointed bridge?

What are these words

But hack street words?

To be mocked

To be deciphered

And the shamed?

Maybe it is not here,

But in those mountains,

Alleyways, places

I have never been nor will never see

That this interloper

Can lie in exile?

She stares out into the alps

And it is filled

With a tender beauty.

## Budapest

*2015 · Mingji Liu*

The Országház. House of the Nation.  
A cathedral of teeth, a fortress of claws.  
A half-buried ribcage, dipped in crimson, sharpened with black ink.  
A redback stalking the banks of the Danube.

The Magyars once built an empire on this bleak, frost sluiced land. Surely with this great war machine, this red-and-white nightmare engine, they can reclaim their mighty kingdom.

## Clay

*2015 · Mingji Liu*

last night

I caught you  
with clay-smeared lips  
and chalk-dust teeth

I caught you  
with a mouthful of sand  
and mortar weeping  
down your chin

in silent awe

I watched you  
suck the marrow from the brickwork with your ragged lips  
pick the limescale off the tiles with your ragged teeth  
before swallowing them  
one by one  
like lemon drops

I wish I knew what it is like  
to be so hungry

to hold an emptiness  
not even stone and chalk could fill

in harvest times  
we hibernated

filled our pockets with the knucklebones of old lovers  
and waited for the water pipes to freeze over

and when we were together  
collecting the underbrush in our mason jars

we wondered together  
where all the mud  
beneath our feet had gone.

## Dresden

*2015 · Mingji Liu*

I think I shall visit Dresden again. In ten, no, twenty years time.

Perhaps by then the yawning, fallow gaps of Altstadt will have blossomed out, back into its former sandstone glory. Perhaps with time the Frauenkirche will no longer resemble the inside of a Hungarian bathhouse. Perhaps one day Neustadt will escape the clutches of its bourgeois-bohème death spiral. Perhaps.

Ah! Imagine that -- Elbflorenz reborn!

How I would love to waltz down Dresden's wide streets again. I would trace my fingers along the street art and ride the rumbling trams. I would kick through the dead leaves of Alaunpark and wander through the riverside flea market, before sailing down the Elbe itself to find out where all the pretty Saxony girls are.

## For a mother-to-be

*2015 · Mingji Liu*

Your child will be a flower small,  
A blossom to be loved by all.

Perhaps a daisy, soft and bright,  
A newborn of the summer light.

It will grow with earth and air,  
And loving parents, kind and near.

The Low Woods is a temperate forest located west of the Craven Sea. It stretches from the northernmost reaches of the Winewaters to the foot of the Spicer Hills.

The forest is composed of a rich array of deciduous and evergreen species such as monk pine, crow's pine, seneschal pine, ironwood, hinterwood, wolfbeam, and blue spear.

It is a gleaming, blameless bethel; its bridges strung with prayer flags, its gutters gorged with chrism, its alleyways carved with eyeless shrines.

Only ten thousand worshippers are allowed within its sacred borders each day. No one is allowed to die or give birth behind its walls.

## Fragments 10-16

*2015 · Mingji Liu*

**(10)**

tonight,  
there is lightning in the skyline

**(11)**

When I was six, the old missionary taught me how to read the symbols carved into the limestone. He would take me up to the outcrops and point out all the carvings. Those rocky hills were like wind-battered islands to me, lost in a sea of yellow tussock, humming of mystery.

*See this one? *He would say, running his hands over a notched and chiselled boulder.  *This one is Cidel*. *The crone star.*

*And this one here,* *the one that looks like a cracked egg*? *This is* *Greni. The formless womb.*

*And look up there. That one is Narin. The night sister. Do you see how it resembles moth wings?*

*Or how about this one? This one is Harna. The snake father.  *The missionary would tap the ancient carving with his walking stick.  *That one, *he would say,* the shepherds still worship that one.  
*

a cyclist  
cuts through melbourne cemetery,  
across a field of headstones  
knocked like crooked teeth.

your helmetless hair dances

**(13)**

Matteo loved watching the ships arrive at port. While his brothers played stick-swords down by the canals, or chased cats and girls in the market square, Matteo would instead run down to the harbour and witness the endless mercantile carnival -- the swaggering stevedores; the rolling barrels; the creaking crates; the snapping ropes; the yelps and cries of foreign tongues.

Every day, the port would be filled with swarms of skiffs, carracks, lateen-sail dhows, clippers and iron-hulled windjammers. He watched men unload peculiar cargos. Red timber and goldwood from the Low Woods. Whale meat and charred idols from the Sevenseas. Pungent spices and scorching peppers from the Eastern Reaches. The marvels and treasures of a hundred nations.

The harbour writhed in a heady, exotic cacophony and Matteo was transfixed.

Occasionally, ambassadorial ships from far-flung, semi-mythical places like Venice and Kolkata and Tenochtitlan would arrive, to sign treaties or draft trading charters. One time he even laid eyes on a great Venetian chattel ship, silently moored at dawn, unloading dozens upon dozens of grim, tattooed slaves. Amidst the early morning sea fog, it looked like a hulk of pale leather, bobbing beside the gantry cranes.

The remaining hors d'oeuvres were being served. Bowls of fried slater, buttered crickets, sweet cicadas, bitter grasshoppers, and iced beetles. The night had barely started and they were already down to the last bottle of the moth brandy.

## paperback

*2015 · Mingji Liu*

This thunderheaded morning  
dribbles down the flimsy eaves  
onto my clammy, crow beak shoes  
as I cross the diluvian bike path and cram  
into a Route 55 tin of weary sardines  
heavy with the recycled breaths of  
so many wet dog commuters --  
the rejected cyclists,  
the dejected Bourke St suits --  
all dripping their thumbs on screens  
that seem to shudder under this tinny pressure,  
and even as I tread for air with musty, metal breaths,  
and even as I stare at the defeated floor,  
I am clenched by this sodden pareidolia  
of seeing your trembling face in the glass drops  
(or your stoplight lips in the window smears)  
and so consumed am I by this sopping,  
gripping, slip and slop of bodies,  
like lukewarm teabags, bobbing on and off,  
making sweaty infusions at Royal Park Station,  
Children's Hospital, at Haymarket,  
that at this stop I am ready to  
plunge myself into the storm  
and curse and curse  
and curse at your rainhead!

Then at Flagstaff Gardens  
A man gets on  
And opens a novel.

I am fixated and stunned  
By how crisp and clean  
The pages are.

And suddenly  
I am calmed by  
The simplicity of things.** **

## Rangatira

*2015 · Mingji Liu*

I can never be defeated.

When my father is eaten, when my brothers are eaten,  
When my sisters are food for beetles,  
And my children are slaves,  
Only I will remain.** **

I can never be silenced.

Strike me with your taiaha, bash me with your patu,  
Pierce me with your Pākehā bullets,  
And you will hear the wind whistle through my chest,  
A musket hole, ragged and raw,  
A hole for bleeding.

** **I will stand strong.

This great forest is my strength, this wild river is my strength,  
This blind woman is my strength.  
If I die today, the forest will keep standing.  
So I will not die today.

## silver skink reserve

*2015 · Mingji Liu*

the mayflies are suspended

above the darkling grass

like sycamore seeds in

botanical vials.

Gingerly, I hike (stumble?)

In this lonesome jacket

Through the damp

heavy, pastoral fields of

urban yellow grass

to I find my spot.

I light a match.

It's a hiss, a flare to signal my

Descretation of the body

Temple.

The clarion hiss of

Barbarians on the steppes.

*Why do I come here?*

*Is it to find this secret place of*

*Peace?*

(A lie)

The drone of the

Medical research building

Far away is snores and mores

And tired howls.

The sky is pinkscale wallpaper

It is a murder of red

And purple, garish

Like the vulva of oyster

*Hush hush*

*My mind is wandering.*

*This tobacco calms me.*

(Damnable lies!)

Taking the match to

My pipe,

I make a charring light.

And squat on this

Shit-stained rock

This twisted, moulting

branch

In the silver skink reserve

The skinks are all asleep.

And I am smoking my pipe.

Watching this violent

Sunset murder.

Mayflies

Suspended like

Sycamore seeds in a vial.

The sunset murder

Mimics the fire of my pipe.

I come here because

There is a sigil on the nape of my

Neck which

Fears that the pretty girls

From across the street

Are judging my out-of-vogue habit

*Therapy of the soul!*

*Squatting on this shit-stained rock!*

*Camphor hurrah!*

Only there are no  
more pretty girls in this part of town.

They all moved south of the river

With their boys and

Their cleanly lungs.

Only the mayflies

And the long grass

And the smoke from my briar pipe.

## This Brunswick house

*2015 · Mingji Liu*

This old Brunswick house:  
Half-swept,  
Half-bones,  
Groans in courtly,  
Nightly,  
Pale display.

A light,  
Transgressively so,  
Curdles its doeskin walls,  
Its dusted floors,  
With pallid and imperial  
Invitation.

Winter arrives,  
As it always does,  
Wool-breathed and horned,  
Star-creamed and dawned,  
Its raw knots unfurled  
Into puffs of silver,  
Into cups of bitter skin.

Yet those were the nights when we  
Held close our starry marriage  
Inside this hollowed out  
Half-way home.

Then came spring,  
That sweet-smelling canton of petals.

Bristled,  
Pinecone green,  
Its red buds yearning  
From the cracks in our stone steps.

Bursting,  
Yellow-gold and gilded,  
A libation of grass seeds and summer dust  
Pouring into our eyes,  
Our lungs,  
Our lust.

Those were the days when we  
Spoke of leaving this wisping,  
Curtained place.

But we never did.  
Or rather,  
We never could.

This old Brunswick house:  
Yours and mine,  
Half-formed,  
Half-swept,  
Half-found.

## Venice

*2015 · Mingji Liu*

Nightfall in Venezia. One must depart the narrow street-squeeze of San Marco and San Polo and escape sideways into the depths of this city. Weaving through the empty canals and corridors of Cannaregio and Castello, one becomes a wanderer among the dim-lit walkways and dead-end bridges, among the snoring sound of boat-creak and lap-lapping of stone steps.

Up ahead, a little square surrounded by the shutters of old shops. They have not yet taken down the Christmas decorations. The street lamps cast a mulled and swollen haze of orange over this place. Two children play soccer against a brick wall.

Have I found the real Venice? Or is this just another trick of the light?
