*Short travel notes salvaged from, 2018–2023. Anything already covered elsewhere on this site has been omitted.*

## 25 August 2023 — Madrid

Paintings from the "Golden Triangle of Art" in Madrid. Aren't El Greco and Francisco Goya just wonderful?

Also, there's nothing quite like standing in a gallery room dedicated entirely to Goya's infamous, mesmerising "Black Paintings".

Also, also, I finally got to see Picasso's Guernica in real life and it was as big and loud and awesome and a bit dumb as I thought it would be.

## 15 August 2023 — The National Gallery and Tate Britain

The National Gallery and Tate Britain. Contains one of my all-time favourite paintings: "An Experiment on a Bird in the Air Pump" by Joseph Wright of Derby. Has two of my all-time favourite (white, male, asshole) British artists: J.M.W. Turner and Francis Bacon. Good stuff.

## 24 March 2020 — Coming home

Since a few of you have been asking, here's a brief and belated update from me: I safely landed in Australia a few days ago and I'm currently in mandatory self-isolation.

How rapidly things have changed for the worse. It was perhaps less than three weeks ago that I sitting on a beach in Egypt, debating where to go next, hoping that I could still eke some travel out of a planet that was shutting its doors. And I was devastated when I finally decided to pull the plug on my trip --- the last couple of months on the road have been some of the happiest of my life.

But looking back on what has transpired in the last week, it's clear that I made the right choice in returning. We are living in unprecedented times. This is undoubtedly the Big One: the upending of things that every single one of us will remember for the rest of our lives. I, for one, am simply relieved to be on a relatively stable footing for now.

Regardless of border closures and travel bans, I doubt I could've continued my travels anyway... Simply the stress of following the deteriorating news cycle was wearing me down and straining my overseas experience. I want to see the world at its best, not at its most fearful. There's little to be gained from exploring a lonely world, a world of shuttered fronts and empty streets. There's no point to that. No point at all.

But make no mistake: I haven't cancelled my world trip, merely placed it on hiatus. The moment this crisis is over, however long that may take, I'll be immediately back on the road. But for now, all that any of us can do is bunker down and weather the storm until it passes.

And it will. That's the truth which needs to cut through the endless cycles of uncertainty. Tough times are ahead for many of us, myself included, and those times may linger, but that's all they'll eventually be: a thin string of moments in time. Of this I am certain. The rain will begin to die down. The howl of the wind will begin to fade. And one day the storm will pass. It will pass.

## 21 June 2019 — Bendigo

Took a trip to Bendigo recently. Saw lots of bats. I think I prefer the gold rush era architecture here to Ballarat? A visit to the Golden Dragon Museum on Chinese immigrant history in the area reminds you why you should never, ever trust white people.

## 7 April 2019 — Art Gallery of New South Wales

Further snaps of arty art from the Art Gallery of NSW. Time for some edgy, sweeping statements: Lucian Freud is the best portraitists ever. Military paintings are fine as a sometimes indulgence. Contemporary Aboriginal art is the most interesting stuff this country produces. Fight me.

## 4 April 2019 — Heaven and earth in Chinese art

More photos from the "Heaven and earth in Chinese art" exhibition at the Art Gallery of NSW. A small, but wonderful exhibition on loan from the National Palace Museum of Taipei.

Check it out if you are in Sydney and have the chance - it's very rare that Chinese artworks of such high quality make it down on tour to little old Australia.

## 30 March 2019 — The ANZAC War Memorial, Sydney

Swung by the ANZAC War Memorial in Hyde Park, Sydney near sundown. I liked the memorial's dignified Art Deco style... the lights solemnly reflecting off the water... the clacking of skateboarders around the memorial grounds... wait, what? Damn kids!

## 19 March 2019 — Escher X nendo, continued

More from the NGV special exhibit: "Escher X nendo | Between Two Worlds". nendo is a "chic" Japanese design studio while MC Escher is, well, the one and only MC Escher.

I actually didn't think the two were a natural fit and the nendo stuff, with the repetitive house motif, felt a bit forced and annoying towards the end. But anyway, that's just my spicy art critique.

## 18 March 2019 — Escher X nendo at the NGV

Some snaps from the NGV's "Escher X nendo | Between Two Worlds" special exhibit, featuring everyone's favourite mind bending Dutch graphic artist. Made me appreciate his visionary skills all over again. Please insert your own *MC Escher, that's my favourite MC* jokes.

## 10 March 2019 — A Chinese temple in Footscray

I've always wondered - while riding the train out west to Footscray, Melbourne - why there was a random Chinese temple by the Maribyrnong River. Turns out it's a temple to Mazu, a sea goddess popular throughout Southeast Asia and various coastal Chinese communities. She's supposed to roam the open seas, protecting sailors from the perils of the ocean. A very handy deity to have around.

## 9 March 2019 — The Waikato River

The Waikato River (which means "Flowing Water" in Maori) in North Island, New Zealand. It is the longest river in the country, snaking indolently from Lake Taupo, through Hamilton, and finally into the Tasman Sea. The Waikato region is a food bowl for NZ and this was the case well before European settlement - with the river being historically used by local Maori tribes to transport produce far and wide.

## 2 March 2019 — The State Library of Victoria

On a blisteringly sunny day, the State Library of Victoria is a classy place to sit and cool down. A true Melbourne icon. Back when it was first opened in 1856 during the Gold Rush, the rule was that all "parties using the library should come with clean hands" and you could be booted out otherwise (according to a newspaper article at the time). You don't want grubby fingers mucking all those fancy books, you see.

## 10 January 2019 — The Taiwanese honour guard

Don't mess with the Taiwanese honour guard. Their bayonets look pointy!

It should be quickly mentioned that the memorial hall honours Chiang Kai-shek, a deeply divisive historical figure who was the leader of mainland China until 1949. After being defeated by the Communists during the Chinese Civil War, his government fled to Taiwan, whereupon he became dictator of Taiwan until his death in 1975.

## 27 December 2018 — Cactus Jack’s, Townsville

## 26 December 2018 — Fort Complex, Magnetic Island

Peeking through old WW2 observation posts on Magnetic Island in Queensland. It's a hot, scraggy hike up to the island's Fort Complex and, if you're lucky, you'll spot a dozing koala or two on the way. (And if you're very lucky, other helpful hikers will have made stick arrows on the path pointing at the trees they're hiding in!)

## 26 December 2018 — Arcadia Bay, Magnetic Island

Shining sands and swaying palm trees... Arcadia Bay on Magnetic Island, a cosy tropical jewel off the coast of Townsville in North Queensland.

The island's named for the distortionary effects it supposedly had on Captain Cook's ship compass when he sailed past it, although no one since has found anything geomagnetically unusual about the place. Just a load of hogwash apparently... 🧭 🤷🏻‍♂️

## 22 December 2018 — The Great Barrier Reef

Blowing bath bubbles at the Great Barrier Reef. Truly one of the natural wonders of the world, and an ecosystem of paramount importance to Australia. I've seen things down there that I could not believe. Giant clams, dazzling fish, fragile corals, monstrous sea turtles, furtive sharks... Incredible things.

## 20 December 2018 — Out of Cairns

On a boat heading out of Cairns for the Great Barrier Reef. I've signed up for a scuba diving liveaboard: three days and two nights of exploring coral reefs in faraway dive sites. Let's hope the seas are calm and the ocean is wondrous 🦈 🐟 🐠 🐡

## 1 October 2018 — Ballarat

Saw some art in Ballarat. The city has one of the finest collections of cow paintings in the Southern Hemisphere. Ate too much cake. Didn't go to Sovereign Hill because gold is lame.

Favourite Ballarat fact: Lake Wendouree is supposedly named after a local aboriginal word which means 'go away', because when a settler asked a local indigenous woman what the place was called, that's what she said.

## 15 August 2018 — Telstra NATSIAA, Darwin

Stunning artwork from the 2018 Telstra National Aboriginal & Torres Strait Islander Art Awards. Such a cool exhibition at the Museum and Art Gallery of the Northern Territory (MAGNT) in Darwin. This one was my favourite: "Seven Sisters" by the Ken Sisters.

## 24 July 2018 — Nadi, Fiji

Best meal I had in Fiji was, funnily enough, on my final day in the rather prosaic town of Nadi. Grilled fish with coconut cream sauce, cassava, and rourou balls (like spinach balls, but made from taro leaves). They don't really do portion control either.

## 15 July 2018 — The Yasawa Islands

A boat ride on the crystal blue of the South Pacific... These are the Yasawa Islands of Fiji, an archipelago of beautiful volcanic islands, accessible only by catamaran (or sea plane, if you're feeling fancy). The catamaran drops off tourists at island resorts all along the island chain, and each boat stop seems to showcase yet another vision of idyllic Fijian paradise.

## 2 April 2018 — The Canterbury Plains

A view of the Canterbury Plains from Mt Thomas. The old (New Zealand) cliché that it's like a verdant patchwork quilt really does hold true! Farms, farms, farms stretching across the pastoral lowlands and into a dark green horizon...

## 30 March 2018 — A beach near Christchurch

A long, quiet stretch of beach near my hometown of Christchurch, New Zealand. A seemingly endless strand with no people in sight. Maybe you see a retiree walking their dog. Maybe a horse rider passes you by. But mostly, it's just a few footprints, as impermanent as the waves that will dissolve them.

## 26 February 2018 — Brunch in a former prison

A bowl of very blue, very fruity yoghurt at The Glass Den in Coburg. It's a great cafe, but perhaps the most interesting thing about it is that it's situated in the old Pentridge Prison. The prison was active from 1851 to 1997 and had its fair share of notoriety: The remains of bush ranger Ned Kelly were once buried there, and the prison was the site of the last execution ever in Australia (in 1967), as well as an infamous prison fire in 1987. So with that in mind, happy brunching...!

## 16 February 2018 — NGV Triennial

Standing in a room full of giant fibreglass skulls back during the 2017/18 National Gallery of Victoria Triennial. This particularly striking exhibit is "Mass" by Australian artist Ron Mueck and is intended to be a study on human mortality — reminding us of boneyard imagery such as the Catacombs of Paris and sites of genocide around the world. Memento mori. Everything is transient.

## 15 February 2018 — The National Museum of Korea

Tucked away in the National Museum of Korea is a little corner showcasing Korean art of dogs throughout history. This one is a 19th century scroll from the late Joseon Period titled "Fierce Dog". Who's a fierce boy? This dog is! Oh yes, he is!

## 14 February 2018 — Ten Thousand Buddhas Monastery

One of the many quirky looking Buddha statues at the Ten Thousand Buddhas Monastery in Hong Kong. The temple's name is a bit of a loose term, in actuality. Not only is the temple not a monastery (it's managed by normal, non-monkish folks like you and I), it also has nearly 13,000 models of beings who have supposedly attained Buddhahood.

## 11 February 2018 — Hong Kong at night

Visiting family in Hong Kong. The city's colour-soaked nightscape is quite incredible, particularly when reflected off the wine-dark waters of Kowloon Bay. Hong Kong has the most skyscrapers taller than 150m of any city in the world (over 350, compared to around 270 in New York) and its skyline is among my favourites during both night and day.

## 11 February 2018 — The Dome Reading Room

Sitting inside the Dome Reading Room of the State Library of Victoria. A majestic, stately building that also happens to be the oldest public library in Australia. I love the banker's lamps in the reading room — the green was supposedly meant to be a pleasing and soothing for the eyes of hardworking office workers back in the early 20th century.

## 11 February 2018 — Sheep

The famous fluffy-white-cloud-with-legs of Aotearoa/New Zealand. It's hard *not* to come across Bo-Peep and company while hiking around the hills of Canterbury. Though it's worth noting that the Sheep-to-Kiwi ratio of NZ has taken a hit over recent years, falling from 22 🐑 per person in the 1980s to six 🐑 per person these days. Baaaa-lasphemy!
