## 24 March 2020 — Coming home

![Coming home](/assets/travel/odds-7-coming-home.jpg)

Since a few of you have been asking, here's a brief and belated update from me: I safely landed in Australia a few days ago and I'm currently in mandatory self-isolation.

How rapidly things have changed for the worse. It was perhaps less than three weeks ago that I sitting on a beach in Egypt, debating where to go next, hoping that I could still eke some travel out of a planet that was shutting its doors. And I was devastated when I finally decided to pull the plug on my trip --- the last couple of months on the road have been some of the happiest of my life.

But looking back on what has transpired in the last week, it's clear that I made the right choice in returning. We are living in unprecedented times. This is undoubtedly the Big One: the upending of things that every single one of us will remember for the rest of our lives. I, for one, am simply relieved to be on a relatively stable footing for now.

Regardless of border closures and travel bans, I doubt I could've continued my travels anyway... Simply the stress of following the deteriorating news cycle was wearing me down and straining my overseas experience. I want to see the world at its best, not at its most fearful. There's little to be gained from exploring a lonely world, a world of shuttered fronts and empty streets. There's no point to that. No point at all.

But make no mistake: I haven't cancelled my world trip, merely placed it on hiatus. The moment this crisis is over, however long that may take, I'll be immediately back on the road. But for now, all that any of us can do is bunker down and weather the storm until it passes.

And it will. That's the truth which needs to cut through the endless cycles of uncertainty. Tough times are ahead for many of us, myself included, and those times may linger, but that's all they'll eventually be: a thin string of moments in time. Of this I am certain. The rain will begin to die down. The howl of the wind will begin to fade. And one day the storm will pass. It will pass.

## 10 March 2019 — A Chinese temple in Footscray

![A Chinese temple in Footscray](/assets/travel/odds-15-a-chinese-temple-in-footscray.jpg)

I've always wondered - while riding the train out west to Footscray, Melbourne - why there was a random Chinese temple by the Maribyrnong River. Turns out it's a temple to Mazu, a sea goddess popular throughout Southeast Asia and various coastal Chinese communities. She's supposed to roam the open seas, protecting sailors from the perils of the ocean. A very handy deity to have around.

## 9 March 2019 — The Waikato River

![The Waikato River](/assets/travel/odds-16-the-waikato-river.jpg)

The Waikato River (which means "Flowing Water" in Maori) in North Island, New Zealand. It is the longest river in the country, snaking indolently from Lake Taupo, through Hamilton, and finally into the Tasman Sea. The Waikato region is a food bowl for NZ and this was the case well before European settlement - with the river being historically used by local Maori tribes to transport produce far and wide.

## 2 March 2019 — The State Library of Victoria

![The State Library of Victoria](/assets/travel/odds-17-the-state-library-of-victoria.jpg)

On a blisteringly sunny day, the State Library of Victoria is a classy place to sit and cool down. A true Melbourne icon. Back when it was first opened in 1856 during the Gold Rush, the rule was that all "parties using the library should come with clean hands" and you could be booted out otherwise (according to a newspaper article at the time). You don't want grubby fingers mucking all those fancy books, you see.

## 26 December 2018 — Arcadia Bay, Magnetic Island

![Arcadia Bay, Magnetic Island](/assets/travel/odds-22-arcadia-bay-magnetic-island.jpg)

Shining sands and swaying palm trees... Arcadia Bay on Magnetic Island, a cosy tropical jewel off the coast of Townsville in North Queensland.

The island's named for the distortionary effects it supposedly had on Captain Cook's ship compass when he sailed past it, although no one since has found anything geomagnetically unusual about the place. Just a load of hogwash apparently... 🧭 🤷🏻‍♂️

## 11 February 2018 — Hong Kong at night

![Hong Kong at night](/assets/travel/odds-36-hong-kong-at-night.jpg)

Visiting family in Hong Kong. The city's colour-soaked nightscape is quite incredible, particularly when reflected off the wine-dark waters of Kowloon Bay. Hong Kong has the most skyscrapers taller than 150m of any city in the world (over 350, compared to around 270 in New York) and its skyline is among my favourites during both night and day.
